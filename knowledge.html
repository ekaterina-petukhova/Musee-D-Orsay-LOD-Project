Knowledge Organization

This section presents the knowledge-organization layer of the project: a theoretical model of the domain in natural language / graphic form, and its formal conceptual model — an OWL formalisation reusing Schema.org, FOAF, SKOS, Dublin Core Terms and BIBO.

A mind map of the relations between the project's central topic and the entities identified in the source text. The transformation itself — Gare d'Orsay → transformed into → Musée d'Orsay — is drawn as the bright, heavyweight spine of the diagram: everything about the station's origins and decline branches off one hero node, and everything about the museum's later development branches off the other. Dashed nodes mark entities added to enrich the network beyond what is explicitly named in the page.

The formal OWL representation of the theoretical model, in Graffoo notation: classes reused from Schema.org, FOAF and SKOS, connected by object and datatype properties from Schema.org, Dublin Core Terms and BIBO, and reconciled to external authorities via owl:sameAs and skos:exactMatch. The LandmarksOrHistoricalBuildings → Museum relation (dcterms:relation, standing in for transformedInto) is drawn as the bright, heavyweight edge — the one relation the whole ontology turns on.

The eight entity types used across the TEI encoding and the RDF graph, each mapped to a class reused from an existing vocabulary.

PER

Person

Historical agents connected to the design, redesign, or artistic identity of the museum.

foaf:Person · Examples: Victor Laloux, Gae Aulenti, Claude Monet, Paul Gauguin
PLC

Place

Buildings, museums and cities that anchor the narrative geographically and architecturally.

schema:Place (+ LandmarksOrHistoricalBuildings, Museum, City, CivicStructure) · Examples: Gare d'Orsay, Musée d'Orsay, Paris, Louvre Museum
ORG

Organisation

Institutions and groups involved in the transformation or its digital afterlife.

foaf:Organization · Examples: ACT architecture group, Google Arts & Culture
EVT

Event

Historical occurrences that motivate or frame the transformation.

schema:Event · Examples: Exposition Universelle of 1900, opening of the Musée d'Orsay
CON

Concept

Abstract categories used to classify architecture, art movements, and the transformation itself.

skos:Concept · Examples: Beaux-Arts style, Impressionism, Post-Impressionism, adaptive reuse
MOV

Movie

A documentary included as a cultural representation of the museum beyond its institutional role.

schema:Movie / bibo:AudioVisualDocument · Example: One Night in Orsay Museum
WEB

WebSite

A digital resource extending access to the museum beyond the physical building.

schema:WebSite · Example: Google Arts & Culture virtual tour
CW

CreativeWork

The project itself, and other authored works referenced by it.

schema:CreativeWork · Example: this project, dcterms:creator → Ekaterina Petukhova
