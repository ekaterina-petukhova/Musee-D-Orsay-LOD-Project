Knowledge Organization

Exploring the conceptual and theoretical frameworks underpinning our semantic model. The museum is modeled using the event-centric CIDOC Conceptual Reference Model (CRM).

The CIDOC Conceptual Reference Model (CRM) provides an extensible ontology for concepts and information in cultural heritage. In our approach, the Musée d'Orsay is not simply treated as a static E22 Man-Made Object, but rather as the nexus of multiple historical events.

By employing an event-centric methodology, we encapsulate the building's lifecycle through E7 Activity and its subclasses. The initial construction (1898-1900) is modeled as an E12 Production carried out by Victor Laloux. The structural conversion to a museum in the 1980s is another E12 Production event attributed to Gae Aulenti. This temporal modeling allows us to query the states of the building at different points in time using E52 Time-Span.

E22

Man-Made Object

A physical object created by human activity.

Ex: The Musée d'Orsay building, the 'Gare d'Orsay' structure.
E39

Actor

People or groups who can perform actions.

Ex: Victor Laloux (Architect), Gae Aulenti (Architect), French State.
E7

Activity

Actions intentionally carried out by actors.

Ex: The classification of the building as a Historic Monument in 1978.
E12

Production

An activity that results in a new man-made object.

Ex: The construction of the railway station (1898-1900).
E53

Place

A geometric extent in space.

Ex: 1 Rue de la Légion d'Honneur, Paris 7e.
E52

Time-Span

An abstract temporal extent.

Ex: The year 1986, the period from 1848 to 1914.
